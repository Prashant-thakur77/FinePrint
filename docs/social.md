## X (thread)

1/ On 1 June 2026, "MicroStrategy sells any Bitcoin by May 31, 2026?" went from 11c to 81c on the 8-K. At 17:00:16 UTC a clarification landed on Polygon and YES fell from 53c to 0.6c by 17:01.

2/ The 2025 version of that market had the same wording. UMA history: proposed, disputed, proposed, disputed. Resolved NO. The record existed. Nobody put it next to the buy button.

3/ Fine Print does. It recalls precedents from @WalrusFoundation Memory on Sui mainnet, reads rules, prices and on-chain clarifications through @Tatum_io, and pins a cited ruling on the chart. Replayed at 12:20 on 1 June, it rules HIGH and cites the 2025 market.

4/ Bots buy the same ruling for 0.02 USDC over x402 (Sui testnet for now). Rulings are sealed before the outcome, graded after, and a wrong LOW ruling is refunded.

5/ The honest part: on 200 unseen 2026 markets, precedent recall didn't beat rule text alone (0.492 vs 0.510). Look-alikes are mostly the same template, not the same risk. Where the wording is the risk it helped: 0.417 to 0.667 on 12 MSTR-pattern markets.

## LinkedIn

On 1 June 2026, "MicroStrategy sells any Bitcoin by May 31, 2026?" on Polymarket went from 11c to 81c after Strategy's 8-K showed bitcoin sold in late May. At 17:00 UTC the market's owner posted a clarification on Polygon, and YES fell from 53c to 0.6c in two minutes. The same wording had been disputed twice on the 2025 version of the market.

For the Total Recall hackathon I built Fine Print, a check that reads a market's rules against past rulings before you buy. Precedents live in Walrus Memory on Sui mainnet (@Walrus Foundation), and every ruling cites the blobs it used. Rules, minute prices and the on-chain clarification history come through Tatum (@Tatum). Trading bots can buy the same ruling for 0.02 USDC over x402, with a refund when a LOW ruling turns out wrong.

I also published where it falls short. On 200 unseen 2026 markets, half of them disputed, broad precedent recall didn't beat rule text alone, because similar-looking markets are mostly the same template rather than the same risk. Where the wording itself is the risk, it helped, though on a small sample. Every number has its script in the repo.
